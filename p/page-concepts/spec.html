How Zana draws a business page

A demo and a plan, updated with Daniel's rulings of November 21, 2026. Nothing here is in production.

The short version

  • One page per business, drawn from what Zana already knows about it. Barrio 13 CrossFit is the first business on it.
  • One launch. The owner and admin edit their own page from day one in the customizer: style, color, logo, cover photo, sections on or off, contact details, prices, the links list and content pages.
  • Besides the page: a links list for the Instagram bio, and up to 3 content pages.
  • Privacy Policy and Terms are footer links that open a panel, never printed on the page.
  • Still being decided: the "I'm interested in" lead form. It is left out of this demo.

The idea

Think of toy bricks. Zana keeps a box of blocks, a paint set called tokens and motion switches called effects. A page is a recipe: which blocks, in what order, painted how. Business data sits apart, so any recipe fits any business.

Picture itBarrio 13 CrossFit switches over from Afya. Its classes, times and passes are already in Zana, so the schedule and the price list fill in with no typing. The owner opens the customizer, picks a color, uploads the logo and a cover photo, marks the 2 Day Pass "Most popular", and fills in WhatsApp and Instagram. The Instagram bio points to the links list, as it does today.

Two things are missing under all of it.

  • A public read. Nothing in Zana hands a business's week and prices to a visitor who is not logged in.
  • A page drawn on the server. The app draws its screens in the browser, like a shop window that stays dark until you walk in. A link pasted in a chat shows no preview, and the first screen is slow on a phone.

The headline and the pitch are drafts Zana writes from the data. A promise (free first class, no card needed, tax included) shows only when the data says so.

A section with nothing to show is left out, and so is its menu link. Three test accounts prove it: no classes, one class, eight classes with long names.

Languages

  • The business's own words have no second language. The owner writes them once. A business may write both languages in its own text, such as "Day Pass / Pase de un día".
  • Zana's own words stay English first, then Spanish. On the page a visitor can switch them (Book a class, Reservar una clase). On the links list both show on the button.

The parts

Blocks. Seventeen, plus the Book button: menu bar, five heroes (film, slideshow, split, framed photo, three tiles), offer strip, statement, class cards, two schedules, price cards, coaches, reviews, first visit, booking bar, footer.

Tokens. One brand color, with a guard that fixes weak contrast. Light or dark. Spacing, corners and photo look. Type: the headline stays 2.5 to 3 times the body, and the first headline of Film and Mono grows to 4 times on a wide screen.

Effects. Nothing waits hidden for one, so a print, a screen capture or a link preview always gets the whole page.

The line against a website builder

Zana does booking and landing pages, never a site builder. So: one page per business with a fixed list of sections, one links list, and up to 3 content pages. The owner turns sections and buttons on or off.

  • Content pages each have their own address, such as barrio13.zana.fit/barrio-games-2026. They are linked from the footer or the links list, never from the menu bar. Each has a title, formatted text (headings, bold, links), a hero photo with text over it, and one photo with text beside it. No galleries, no layouts.
  • Own links on the links list: up to 3, a name and a web address each, marked as leaving the page.
  • No free text blocks on the page, no custom code, no layout to drag.
Picture itBarrio asks for a "Barrio Games 2026" section. It becomes a content page with its own address, linked from the footer and from the links list. The page itself does not change.

What ships at launch

  • Two styles, which are really one dial. Timetable for a business with classes: the schedule leads. Solo for a trainer on a phone: prices lead, and the page says sessions, never classes.
  • The launch sections: menu bar, hero, schedule, statement, class cards, prices, footer. Plus the Book button, and the cover photo: in the hero on Solo, as a wide band under the hero on Timetable.
  • Three effects: the launch rows above.
  • Prices: the owner's label per product, the "what you get" lines, and which products are featured. The page shows the featured ones, then "See all". Each card says "Taxes included" when the price does.
  • The footer: address, the note under it, WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok and phone (each only when filled in), the content pages, then Privacy Policy and Terms. Those two open a panel with the text from the business's legal documents, and each has its own address too.
  • The links list: logo, name and one line on top. Then Book a class, Prices, Join, Location, WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok and phone (each only when filled in), the content pages, and up to 3 own links.
  • Up to 3 content pages.
  • The customizer. The owner and admin set all of the above themselves.

The customizer

Owners see a recipe as a style. It sets every default, and the owner changes a short list within limits, with a live preview of the page, the links list or a content page. Try it. What it still gets wrong:

  • Eight effect switches should be one Motion control: Full, Calm, Off.
  • Headline size shows numbers. Hide them: small, medium, large is enough.
  • Missing: editing the headline, where a photo is cropped, a share link or QR code, and unpublish.
  • The Solo style is not in it.
  • Section order is the first step across the line against a site builder. It is in the mock, tagged Later. I would cut it.

Fonts: ask this first

It decides how different the styles can be.

  1. Geist only. Zana's rule today. Styles differ by size, weight, color and layout. Calm loses its serif, Mono its tall capitals.
  2. Geist plus one display family. Archivo is one file whose letters slide from tall and narrow to wide. Mono keeps its look and a wide look becomes possible. Calm goes without a serif.
  3. Three families. Calm keeps its serif. Three files to host and load.

My pick is 2. The launch does not wait on it: both launch styles, the links list and the content pages use Geist alone.

What gets built

One launch. The pieces, in the order they depend on each other.

The launch

The public read: week, classes, prices, links list and content pages with no login
The page drawn on the server, so link previews and a fast first screen work. Every view has its own address: the page, /links, each content page, /privacy and /terms

New product details: a label, the "what you get" lines, featured on the page

The customizer with live preview, publish and unpublish
Booking hand-off: Book and a tapped class open booking with that class chosen
Measurement: visits, Book taps and bookings, by source, the links list included

Later

Five more effects

The video bill: list prices today run near $5 a month for each 1,000 minutes stored and $1 for each 1,000 minutes watched. $5 a month holds 3,000 videos of 20 seconds. One of them watched 3,000 times is 1,000 minutes, so about $1 more.

How we know it works

  • A stranger goes from the link to a booked class in under 2 minutes. The first screen shows in under 2.5 seconds on a phone. A pasted link shows the business name, one line and a photo. 5% of page visits end in a booking or a purchase.
  • 70% of new businesses publish in their first week, in under 10 minutes, with no help.
  • Barrio's Instagram bio points to its Zana links list, and taps on it show up in the numbers.
  • If under 2% of visits book after 8 weeks, fix the page before building more of it.
