How Zana draws a business page

A demo and a plan. Nothing here is in production.

The short version

  • One page per business, drawn from what Zana already knows about it.
  • Honest cost: about 14 weeks for one developer. I said 6 before.
  • My pick: build only phase 1 now, about 5 weeks. One renderer, two styles, and Zana staff set the color, logo and cover photo.
  • Three rulings are yours: the launch list, fonts and video.

The idea

Think of toy bricks. Zana keeps a box of blocks, a paint set called tokens and motion switches called effects. A page is a recipe: which blocks, in what order, painted how. Business data sits apart, so any recipe fits any business.

Picture itMarisol opens a pilates studio. Her classes, times and prices are already in Zana, so the schedule and the price list fill in with no typing. Her photos, her class descriptions and the sentence about her studio are not. Somebody has to add those. My first plan left that cost out.

Two things are missing under all of it.

  • A public read. Nothing in Zana hands a business's week and prices to a visitor who is not logged in.
  • A page drawn on the server. The app draws its screens in the browser, like a shop window that stays dark until you walk in. A link pasted in a chat shows no preview, and the first screen is slow on a phone.

The headline and the pitch are drafts Zana writes from the data. Staff read them in phase 1. Owners edit them from phase 2. A promise (free first class, no card needed, tax included) shows only when the data says so.

A section with nothing to show is left out, and so is its menu link. Three test accounts prove it: no classes, one class, eight classes with long names.

The parts

Blocks. Seventeen, plus the Book button: menu bar, five heroes (film, slideshow, split, framed photo, three tiles), offer strip, statement, class cards, two schedules, price cards, coaches, reviews, first visit, booking bar, footer.

Tokens. One brand color, with a guard that fixes weak contrast. Light or dark. Spacing, corners and photo look. Type: the headline stays 2.5 to 3 times the body, and the first headline of Film and Mono grows to 4 times on a wide screen.

Effects. Nothing waits hidden for one, so a print, a screen capture or a link preview always gets the whole page.

The line against a website builder

Zana does booking and landing pages, never a site builder. So: one page per business, a fixed list of sections, and the owner turns the optional ones on or off. No free text blocks, no second page, no custom code, no layout to drag.

Picture itAn owner asks for a "Summer challenge" section. The answer is no. It goes in the offer strip, one line, or it waits.

Section order is the first step across that line. It is in the mock. I would cut it.

What ships first: your ruling

I propose this launch list.

  • Two styles, which are really one dial. Timetable for a gym: the schedule leads. Solo for a trainer on a phone: prices lead, and the page says sessions, never classes.
  • Seven sections: menu bar, hero, schedule, statement, class cards, prices, footer. Plus the Book button.
  • Three effects: the phase 1 rows above.
  • Only what Zana already holds. Zana staff set the color, the logo and one cover photo. No owner screen.

Owner settings

Phase 2. Owners see a recipe as a style. It sets every default, and the owner changes a short list within limits. Try the mock. What it still gets wrong:

  • Eight effect switches should be one Motion control: Full, Calm, Off.
  • Headline size shows numbers. Hide them: small, medium, large is enough.
  • Missing: editing the headline and the offer, Instagram and WhatsApp links, the page's languages, where a photo is cropped, a share link or QR code, and unpublish.
  • The Solo style is not in it.

Fonts: ask this first

It decides how different the styles can be.

  1. Geist only. Zana's rule today. Styles differ by size, weight, color and layout. Calm loses its serif, Mono its tall capitals.
  2. Geist plus one display family. Archivo is one file whose letters slide from tall and narrow to wide. Mono keeps its look and a wide look becomes possible. Calm goes without a serif.
  3. Three families. Calm keeps its serif. Three files to host and load.

My pick is 2. Phase 1 does not wait on it: both launch styles use Geist alone.

The bill: list prices today run near $5 a month for each 1,000 minutes stored and $1 for each 1,000 minutes watched. $5 a month holds 3,000 videos of 20 seconds. One of them watched 3,000 times is 1,000 minutes, so about $1 more.

What it pushes back. Five weeks of one developer moves everything else on the launch list by about 2 weeks. The public read and the hand-off, 1.5 of those 5 weeks, are owed by the public schedule in any case. Phases 2 and 3 are 9 more weeks of one developer, taken from booking, billing, training and messages.

My pick: foundation first, and smaller than I first said.

Phase 1 only, for the first customers. Staff set color, logo and cover by hand: a handful of businesses does not justify a settings screen. Phase 2 when the numbers below ask for it. Phase 3 last, with video bought.

Example: a trainer signs up on a Monday. Staff pick her color and upload her logo and one photo. Ten minutes. Her link works that day: prices first, her open times, Book. She never sees a settings screen.

The case against even phase 1: the first customers need booking and billing to work before they need a prettier window. If the launch list is tight, build only the 1.5 weeks that are owed anyway and show a plain schedule.

How we know it works

  • Phase 1. A stranger goes from the link to a booked class in under 2 minutes. The first screen shows in under 2.5 seconds on a phone. A pasted link shows the business name, one line and a photo. 5% of page visits end in a booking or a purchase.
  • Start phase 2 when 10 businesses have a page, or staff spend more than an hour a week setting pages up. If under 2% of visits book after 8 weeks, fix the page before building more of it.
  • Phase 2. 70% of new businesses publish in their first week, in under 10 minutes, with no help from staff.
