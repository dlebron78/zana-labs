What a member sees when she signs in

A recommendation and a mockup. Nothing here is in production.

The short version

  • My pick: one home, one Book button, one menu. The home runs in time order. A black Book button stays pinned at the bottom. Everything else sits under the gym's name, the way the staff app already works on a phone.
  • I would not build the tab bar. It sorts the app by feature, and a member opens it to do one thing. It shows her workout and her plan in two places. It changes shape from one member to the next. Every new feature will ask for a fifth tab.
  • Your list fits in all three layouts. Plan and buying more, booking, workouts, news, warnings, selling. The difference is that in the pick, Book never scrolls away and an offer can never push it down.
  • The owner sets up nothing for this screen.
  • Nine things the pick needs have no card yet. Three rulings are yours.

The three layouts

  1. Home + Book. My pick. One home in time order: what needs her, what is now, what is next, this week, her plan. One black button pinned at the bottom, Book a class, shown only when she can book. The gym's name at the top opens a short menu.
  2. Bottom tabs. The proposal. Home, Schedule, Workouts and Account in a bar at the bottom. The mockup shows it at its best: a tab hides when this member has nothing for it.
  3. Menu only. The staff app's phone layout as it is today: a home, and a menu under the name. Book is a line in that menu and a row on the home.
Picture itMarta trains on four tracks. Tuesday at 5:40 PM she opens Zana to read tonight's workout and to book Thursday. In the pick she sees the workout and the Book button at the same time. With tabs she sees the workout on Home, again on Workouts, and books on a third tab. With menu only, the Book row sits under four workouts.

Where selling sits

  • One offer at a time. It comes from the gym's own price list, so the owner sets up nothing.
  • She pays one class at a time: the smallest pack. At the gym's intro price, if it has one and she never bought a pack.
  • Her pack has 2 or fewer left: the next pack up, or the same one again when there is no bigger one.
  • She is on a monthly plan: no offer.
  • Where: at her plan card, and under the black button when she is about to pay for one class. That is the moment a pack is worth the most to her.
  • It moves to the top only once: when her pack runs out. In blue, never red, with a "Not now" that sends it back down to her plan.
  • It can never crowd booking. Book is pinned outside the page, so nothing above it can push it down.

The rule for what shows

A thing shows only when this person has one. Nothing is shown empty, locked or grayed out. There is no switch for the owner to set. This is the rule the staff menu already follows (card PHO-358, "Shell sections come from what the gym uses, no switches", Done), applied to one person.

From 3 clients to 4 tracks and kids

  • A solo trainer with 3 clients. A client sees the trainer's name and color, a note, today's program day, the next session and the sessions left. Five lines in the menu. No Book button, no schedule, no news line.
  • The trainer adds one Saturday group class. The Book button and a Schedule line appear for his clients. Nothing else moves.
  • A gym with four tracks and kids. The same home. It gets more rows, not more tabs. Family appears for parents only.

  • The waitlist goes inside the class sheet (card PHO-396, "The Waitlist, member self-serve join and leave"). The mockup shows it on Wednesday at 6:00 PM.
  • A check-in code becomes one row on top on class day (card PHO-36, "The Badge").

  • When a client can book a one-on-one, the same pinned button reads "Book a session" (card PHO-475, "Later: a client books a 1:1 from the coach's open times").
  • Texts change nothing on screen (card PHO-476, "Later: texting and WhatsApp reminders").
  • A shop is one offer line and one menu line. Never a tab.

What the owner sets up

Nothing, for this screen. Four things are optional: a logo (in Settings today), a color for the bar, a news line, and an intro price. With none of them the home still works: a black bar, no news line, and the offer uses the normal pack price.

Where the pick is weak

  • History and personal bests are two taps away: the name, then Workouts. Tabs make it one.
  • Book is pinned on the home only. From another page it is back, then Book. Tabs make it one tap from anywhere.
  • The menu under the name is easy to miss. So nothing she needs every week lives only there.
  • The home is long on a four-track day. Each extra track costs one line.
  • The home needs discipline. One offer. Red only for real trouble. At most two notices showing, the rest behind one line. The mockup does not build that last cap.
  • "Your usual" needs two weeks of bookings. A new member does not get it yet.

What the pick needs that has no card

The member app as a whole sits under card PHO-192, "The final frontend", In Progress. These pieces have no card of their own.

  1. The member home and how she gets around it. This decision.
  2. Her own read of her day. Today's workout exists, but only staff can open it.
  3. Her bookings, and cancelling one herself.
  4. Her plan line and "buy more" from it. Neighbors: PHO-286 "Storefront" and PHO-374 "Design checkout as one piece".
  5. The offer rule. One offer, from the price list. An intro price is new: the price list design has none today.
  6. Paying an overdue plan inside the app. Neighbors: PHO-308 "A client saves her own card from a link" and PHO-454 "Later: send a pay link".
  7. News the gym writes, and the notices on top of the home.
  8. A parent acting for a child in the app: book, pay, see.
  9. The history and personal bests screen.

Smaller, and optional: the "your usual" one-tap rebook, and a color for the bar.

The design rules

The pick starts from the rules Zana already has: black or white says where you are and a color is only a status. The gym's color paints the bar and its menu, nothing else. One black button per screen. Focus by time. One sheet, nothing stacked. An action sits where its object is seen. Undo, not "Are you sure?". The same action keeps the same word: Book.

The rules say the member app's way of getting around is still open. I would close it by adding three rules:

  1. Member app: one home in time order. One pinned black button for her main action, shown only when she can do it. Everything else under the business's name. No tab bar.
  2. Notices: red only when something goes wrong if she ignores it. Blue when it can wait.
  3. Offers: one at a time, from the price list, at the thing it is about. Never red. On top only when a pack runs out.

How we would know it works

These are my proposals. There are no numbers yet to compare them with.

  • Taps from opening Zana to a booked class: 3 or fewer for half of all bookings. 1 for a usual class.
  • Visits that never open the menu: 8 in 10 or more. If it is lower, something is in the wrong place.
  • Bookings she makes herself: 7 in 10, two months after a gym switches. The rest are staff booking for her.
  • Scores logged the same day, among members who came to a track class: 4 in 10 as a first target.
  • Overdue payments paid from the red card: half of them within 2 days.
  • Packs bought again within 3 days of running out: 5 in 10. This one measures the offer.

Your rulings

  1. No tab bar for members. If you still want tabs, the mockup shows the best version I could make. Use the switch at the top.
  2. The selling rule, and whether a gym may set an intro price on a first pack.
  3. One tap books when it costs her nothing more, with Undo. When a booking charges her, the price always shows first.

What I checked and what I assumed

Checked. I read the production code and the design rules, without changing them, and the cards. I ran every screen of the mockup for the four members, the three layouts and both languages, about 6,000 screens, looking for errors and missing words. I ran a scripted tap-through of each member's steps, about 400 checks. I read the layout code at phone width (390) and laptop width (1280).

Not seen. I had no browser, so nobody has looked at this page with their own eyes yet. Spacing and line breaks may need a pass.

Assumed.

  • A booking holds one class from a pack, and cancelling gives it back.
  • A gym can pick a color for the bar. Settings has a logo and "Black or Light" today.
  • A gym can set an intro price on a first pack.
  • "Your usual" comes from her last two weeks of bookings.
  • Cancelling shows no fee and no refund, because those rules are not decided (card PHO-404, "No-shows and late-cancel penalties", Backlog).
  • The Spanish is a first pass. It says "Entreno" for workouts and "relevo" for waiver.
  • Tablado and its people are made up. A web search found no gym of that name in Puerto Rico. Repecho is the labs' own sample trainer.
